# Story: why people keep watching

A video is not a list of things to show. It makes a promise in the first second and keeps it by the last one. Plan the story before you pick a single model.

## 1. Find the promise

Write one sentence: what should the viewer feel, know or want when it ends? ("This café makes the best cold coffee in town." "Look how my sister's year went." "You can fix this in three steps.") Every shot, line and sound serves that sentence. If something doesn't, cut it — even if the user mentioned it.

Reorder freely. The order the user told you things is rarely the best order to show them. Lead with what the viewer gets, not with background.

## 2. The hook (the first 1–2 seconds)

Most viewers decide in the first second, often with the sound off. The first frame must already be interesting, and the main idea should be readable on screen within the first two seconds.

| Hook | Use when | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Result first | There's a satisfying "after" | Open on the finished room, then show the mess it was |
| Bold claim | The point is surprising | "This cheap spice beats the expensive one." |
| Question | The viewer already wonders about it | "Why does street chai taste better?" |
| Mid-action | There's motion to drop into | Start as the dough hits the pan — no lead-in |
| Direct address | The audience is specific | "If you run a small bakery, watch this." |
| Before → after tease | A transformation | Two seconds of the old photo, a flash, the new one |
| Pattern break | Feeds all look the same | Something unexpected: a car parked on a rooftop |

Never open on a logo, a slow fade from black, a blank title card or "Hi guys". The logo belongs at the end.

## 3. Pick one shape

| Shape | Beat order | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem → fix | hook → the pain → the solution → proof it works → call to action | Ads, services, apps |
| Before → after | before → the change happening → after → reaction or payoff | Makeovers, edits, fitness, homes |
| Show → explain → show | the result → how it's made → the result again, bigger | Food, crafts, products |
| Story | setup → something changes → it gets harder → the turn → payoff | Short films, brand stories |
| List | hook → item 1 … item N (best one last) → wrap | Tips, places, mistakes |
| Steps | the goal → step 1 … step N on the same "stage" → done | How-to, recipes, tutorials |
| Mood montage | build → peak → release | Travel, events, music videos, brand films |

Don't switch shapes halfway. A list that turns into a story loses people.

## 4. One job per beat

Write a beat sheet before choosing models. Each beat has exactly one job; if you can't name the job, the beat goes.

| # | Job | What we see | Words (on screen or spoken) | Seconds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hook | Close-up: ice cracking into cold coffee | "Hot outside?" | 1.5 |
| 2 | Promise | Medium: the barista slides the glass forward | "We fixed that." | 1.5 |
| 3 | Proof | Top-down: three signature drinks | "3 house specials" | 2.5 |
| … | | | | |
| 8 | Call to action | The café front at dusk, logo | "Open till 11 pm" | 2.5 |

## 5. Keep the middle moving

- **Escalate.** Each beat is a little closer, bigger or more surprising than the last. Put the best shot near the end, not first.
- **Change something every few seconds** — a new angle, place, line of text or musical hit. Too long without change reads as "this is over".
- **Breathe before the peak.** A slightly longer or quieter shot right before the climax makes the climax land.

## 6. End on purpose

- Land the promise, then give **one** call to action (follow, visit, order, link in bio). Two asks get neither.
- Put the final line or logo on the last strong music hit and hold it for 1.5–2 seconds.
- For Reels and Shorts that replay, a last shot that flows into the first makes people watch twice.

## 7. Writing the words

- Short spoken lines: about 6–20 words, one idea each, plain words, active verbs. People speak about 2.5 words per second — a 15-second video holds roughly 35 spoken words.
- Write in small cues the pictures can follow: "Fresh beans. Slow pour. Your morning, sorted." Each cue is a moment for a cut or a word on screen.
- Skip hype and filler: "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world".
- If the user gave their own script, keep their words and only cut it into beats. Rewrite only if they ask.
- Silence is allowed. A shot with no words often hits harder.

## 8. Length

| Where it's posted | Typical length |
| --- | --- |
| Reels, TikTok, Shorts | 7–30 s (ads: 6, 15 or 30 s; stories and tutorials up to 60 s) |
| Feed posts (1:1, 4:5) | 10–30 s |
| YouTube and websites (16:9) | Intros 5–10 s; promos 30–90 s |

Shorter is almost always better. If it can be said in 15 seconds, don't make 30.
